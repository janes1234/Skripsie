Know for the oral:
   do the padded zeros influence normalisation? Should they?

   Short answer: No, the padded zeros don't affect the normalisation constants, and they shouldn't. They do reach two other steps that normalise or rescale, though, and the report doesn't mention either. Neither looks serious.

What the code does

make_transform in CNN/wwtw_utils.py:127 runs these steps in order:

1. LetterboxResize (pads with black, value 0)
2. Augmentation: flip, rotation, colour jitter
3. ToTensor
4. Normalize(IMAGENET_MEAN, IMAGENET_STD)

The mean and std are the fixed ImageNet values (wwtw_utils.py:67-68). Nothing is computed from your images, so the amount of padding can't change them. After normalisation a padded pixel becomes about (−2.12, −2.04, −1.80). Every black pixel gets that value, whether it comes from padding, the clarifier mask or the rotation corners.

Should they influence it?

No. If you worked out a mean and std from your own dataset, the padding and masks would pull them down: about 22% of each clarifier input and 19% of each aerobic zone input is black. But for the four pretrained models, using ImageNet statistics is correct because the backbones expect that input distribution. The scratch ResNet-18 could have used dataset statistics, but the first conv layer and BatchNorm absorb a fixed per-channel affine transform, so it hardly matters.

The report's wording is accurate (Chapter5.tex:75): it pads first, then normalises with the ImageNet mean and std "since the pretrained backbones were trained on inputs normalised this way." That matches the code.
