\mychapter{Summary and conclusions}{Summary and Conclusions}{}\label{chap:conclusion}

\section{Summary}
\label{sec:conc_summary}

This project investigated whether a CNN can determine the state of the aerobic zones and secondary clarifiers of a WWTW from Google Earth images. Five models were trained for each component and evaluated on a chronological split, which represents a known facility, and on a leave-one-facility-out split, which represents a new facility (Chapter~\ref{chap:experimental_design}).

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conc_conclusions}

When classifying images from a known facility, the clarifier models reach a mean AUC of 0.921 to 0.949 and the aerobic zone models 0.860 to 0.938 (Table~\ref{tab:res_multiclass}), meaning that the state of both components can be determined with good accuracy from satellite images. When classifying images from a new facility, the clarifier models perform about as well, but the macro recall of the aerobic zone models decreases by 12.2 to 30.5 percentage points. An aerobic zone model should therefore only be used to classify images from a facility that is part of the training data, which is the situation in the intended practical application of the system. As binary classifiers, the models reach or come close to the target Not-OK recall of 95\% at a known facility, but do not reach this target at a new facility (Table~\ref{tab:res_binary}). The Grad-CAM heatmaps mostly identify features that agree with human assessments, but not in all cases. 

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:conc_limitations}

The aerobic zone labels were not reviewed, so some of the aerobic zone classification errors may be due to incorrect labels. The aerobic zones are also labelled at only three facilities, so their leave-one-facility-out results are based on only three test sets. Finally, the hyperparameters were tuned only with Northern Works held out for the clarifiers and Atlantis held out for the aerobic zones, and the same values were used in every experiment, so they may not be optimal for the other facilities.

\section{Future Work}
\label{sec:conc_future}

The next step is to implement a dashboard, where new images can be classified, and components classified as Not-OK can be relabelled by an operator, and the relabelled images added to the training data. The classification may also be improved with satellite data that has more spectral bands, such as near-infrared, or a higher resolution than the RGB images from Google Earth.
