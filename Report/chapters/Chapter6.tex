\mychapter{Results}{Results}{}\label{chap:results}

\section{Overview}
\label{sec:results_overview}

The results are presented for two situations. In the first, the images of a facility have been relabelled and are part of the training data. Experiment~1 gives an upper bound for this situation, since its test images were not taken after its training images, and some of them come from the same satellite image as a training image (Section~\ref{sec:exp_mixed}). In the second, a facility has been added and none of its images have been labelled. Experiment~2 estimates the performance in this situation.

Section~\ref{sec:results_multiclass} gives the multi-class results, Section~\ref{sec:results_binary} the binary OK / Not-OK results at a Not-OK recall of 95\%, and Section~\ref{sec:results_gradcam} the Grad-CAM heatmaps of the two selected models. The full results are given in Appendix~\ref{appen:additional_results}. In every table, the best value in every column is shown in bold.

Table~\ref{tab:res_multiclass} shows that the macro F1 score decreases from Experiment~1 to Experiment~2 for every architecture, by at most 14.6 percentage points for the clarifiers and by at least 26.0 percentage points for the aerobic zones. The aerobic zone models therefore generalise worse to a new facility than the clarifier models. ConvNeXt-Tiny has the highest clarifier F1 score and DenseNet-121 the highest aerobic zone F1 score in both experiments. In Experiment~2, the pretrained clarifier architectures differ by less than the standard deviation across facilities of any one of them, so the test facility has a larger effect on the result than the architecture.

\section{Qualitative Analysis: Grad-CAM}
\label{sec:results_gradcam}

The Grad-CAM method and example selection described in Section~\ref{sec:exp_gradcam} were applied to the ConvNeXt-Tiny clarifier model, tested on Fisantekraal, and the DenseNet-121 aerobic zone model, tested on Waterval (Figure~\ref{fig:res_cm_examples}). The last convolutional layers of these networks have a spatial size of $7\times7$ and $3\times7$ respectively, so the heatmaps are coarse.

\subsection{Clarifiers}
\label{sec:results_gradcam_clarifiers}

Figure~\ref{fig:gradcam_clarifier} shows the clarifier examples. For the correctly classified \textbf{Dysfunctional}, \textbf{Empty} and \textbf{Scum} clarifiers, the highest weights lie on the surface inside the tank: on the green surface, on the shadow and floor of the empty tank, and on the surface next to the bridge, respectively. For the \textbf{Functional} clarifier, the highest weight lies where the bridge meets the rim of the tank, with lower weights on the surface.

The ConvNeXt-Tiny heatmaps highlight one or two small regions rather than a large part of the surface. A possible cause is the global max pooling of ConvNeXt-Tiny (Section~\ref{sec:exp_architectures}), which passes only the largest activation of each feature map to the classifier head.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.85\textwidth]{figures/gradcam_clarifier.png}
    \caption{Grad-CAM heatmaps of the ConvNeXt-Tiny clarifier model, for test images from Fisantekraal, which was held out during training. Top: one correctly classified clarifier per class. Bottom: one clarifier from each of the four most common mistakes. Every heatmap is calculated for the predicted class, and $p$ is the softmax probability of that class. Red regions contributed the most to the prediction.}
    \label{fig:gradcam_clarifier}
\end{figure}

In the \textbf{Scum} clarifier classified as \textbf{Functional} ($p = 0.55$), the light patch in the centre of the tank receives little weight, and the highest weight lies on the black surface near the edge of the tank. This region resembles a \textbf{Functional} clarifier, but the class requires the whole surface to be black. In the \textbf{Functional} clarifier classified as \textbf{Scum}, the image is grainy and has a high contrast, and the highest weight lies on the surface at the left edge of the tank. The noise in the image may have been taken for an uneven surface.

Of the 17 \textbf{Dysfunctional} clarifiers at Fisantekraal, 9 were misclassified. In both examples, most of the weight lies on or next to the rim of the tank, and for the clarifier classified as \textbf{Functional}, part of it lies on the background outside the tank. Neither region indicates the state of the clarifier. The surface of this clarifier is also pale grey-green rather than the darker green of the correctly classified example.

\subsection{Aerobic Zones}
\label{sec:results_gradcam_aerobic}

Figure~\ref{fig:gradcam_aerobic} shows the aerobic zone examples. For the correctly classified \textbf{Functional} zone ($p = 1.00$), the heatmap covers the centre of the zone, where the leopard print pattern is visible. The correctly classified \textbf{Dysfunctional} and \textbf{Suboptimal} zones have $p = 0.49$ and $p = 0.43$, slightly above the $0.33$ of a random guess between three classes. Their heatmaps lie on the smooth surface at the left end of the zone and on a darker, uneven band in the right half of the zone, respectively.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.9\textwidth]{figures/gradcam_aerobic_zone.png}
    \caption{Grad-CAM heatmaps of the DenseNet-121 aerobic zone model, for test images from Waterval, which was held out during training. Top: one correctly classified aerobic zone per class. Bottom: one aerobic zone from each of the three most common mistakes. Every heatmap is calculated for the predicted class, and $p$ is the softmax probability of that class. Red regions contributed the most to the prediction.}
    \label{fig:gradcam_aerobic}
\end{figure}

In the \textbf{Suboptimal} zone classified as \textbf{Functional}, the left and centre of the zone show a leopard print pattern and the right third is smooth. The heatmap lies on the patterned centre, and the smooth part receives little weight. A zone is \textbf{Suboptimal} when a third or more of its surface shows no leopard print (Chapter~\ref{chap:systemmethods}), so the class depends on how much of the surface is patterned, which the model did not take into account. This zone is close to the boundary between the two classes, and the aerobic zone labels were not reviewed (Chapter~\ref{chap:systemmethods}), so some of these mistakes may be caused by the labels.

The \textbf{Dysfunctional} zone classified as \textbf{Functional} ($p = 0.50$) has a smooth surface with no visible pattern, and its heatmap is spread over a large part of the zone. For the \textbf{Dysfunctional} zone classified as \textbf{Suboptimal}, the heatmap lies on a darker patch at the right end of the zone. Neither region resembles the leopard print pattern.

\subsection{Summary of Observations}
\label{sec:results_gradcam_summary}

For confident, correct predictions, the heatmaps lie on the features that the labels are based on. For \textbf{Scum} and \textbf{Suboptimal}, which depend on how much of the surface shows a feature, the mistakes are based on the region where the feature is strongest, and the rest of the surface receives little weight. When the surface contains little evidence, the heatmaps spread out or move to the rim of the tank, and the confidence is low. These observations are based on a few examples, but they agree with the most common mistakes at both facilities.


\section{Summary of Findings}
\label{sec:results_summary}

\begin{itemize}
    \item The clarifier models perform better than the aerobic zone models in both experiments. In Experiment~2, the highest macro F1 score is 0.774 for the clarifiers (ConvNeXt-Tiny) and 0.498 for the aerobic zones (DenseNet-121).
    \item At a new facility, the macro F1 score decreases by at most 14.6 percentage points for the clarifiers and by at least 26.0 percentage points for the aerobic zones.
    \item The differences between the pretrained architectures are smaller than the variation across held-out facilities.
    \item At a new facility, the recall decreases most for the \textbf{Empty} clarifiers and for both aerobic zone failure classes. The most common mistake for both components is a failure class classified as \textbf{Functional}.
    \item At a Not-OK recall of 95\%, the highest specificity decreases from 0.877 to 0.528 for the clarifiers and from 0.776 to 0.409 for the aerobic zones from Experiment~1 to Experiment~2.
    \item A threshold shared across facilities does not give a consistent recall or specificity at individual facilities.
    \item The Grad-CAM heatmaps of correct, confident predictions lie on the features that the labels are based on. Mistakes for classes that depend on how much of the surface shows a feature are based on the region where the feature is strongest.
\end{itemize}
