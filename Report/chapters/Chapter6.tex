\mychapter{Results}{Results}{}\label{chap:results}

\section{Overview}
\label{sec:results_overview}

The results are presented for two situations. In the first, which is the usual situation in the intended practical application of the system, earlier images of a facility have been labelled and are part of the training data. Experiment~1 estimates the performance in this situation, in which all of the test images are taken chronologically after the training images (Section~\ref{sec:exp_mixed}). The second situation reflects the scenario where the test images are from a new facility that is not in the training set. Experiment~2 estimates the performance in this situation.

Section~\ref{sec:results_multiclass} gives the multi-class results for experiments 1 and 2, Section~\ref{sec:results_binary} the binary OK / Not-OK results at a threshold chosen for a Not-OK recall of 95\% on the development set, Section~\ref{sec:results_augmentation} the effect of augmentation, and Section~\ref{sec:results_gradcam} presnets the Grad-CAM heatmaps of the two selected models. A complete set of results is given in Appendix~\ref{appen:additional_results}. 


\section{Multi-Class Classification}
\label{sec:results_multiclass}

In the multi-class results, each test image is assigned to the class with the highest softmax probability, so no threshold is used, and the macro recall is the unweighted mean of the recalls of the classes (Sections~\ref{sec:metrics_background} and~\ref{sec:exp_metrics}). Table~\ref{tab:res_multiclass} shows that the test set macro recall of the aerobic zones decreases from Experiment~1 to Experiment~2 for every architecture, by between 12.2 and 30.5 percentage points, and the mean AUC by between 13.5 and 35.8 percentage points. For the clarifiers, the change in macro recall ranges from a decrease of 8.5 to an increase of 3.4 percentage points, and the change in mean AUC from a decrease of 3.8 to an increase of 0.4 percentage points, so the clarifier models perform about as well at a new facility as they do on new images from a known facility. The aerobic zone models therefore generalise less well to a new facility than the clarifier models. 

In Experiment~1, the \textbf{Empty} class has the lowest recall of the clarifier failure classes for every architecture (Table~\ref{tab:res_failure_recall}). DenseNet-121 has the highest clarifier macro recall in Experiment~1 and EfficientNet-B0 in Experiment~2, while ConvNeXt-Tiny has the highest clarifier mean AUC in both. EfficientNet-B0 has the highest aerobic zone macro recall in Experiment~1 and DenseNet-121 in Experiment~2, while ConvNeXt-Tiny has the highest aerobic zone mean AUC in Experiment~1 and DenseNet-121 in Experiment~2. In Experiment~2, the standard deviation of the clarifier macro recall across facilities (0.062 to 0.097) is of the same magnitude as the differences between the pretrained architectures (0.648 to 0.749), so the test facility has about as large an effect on the result as the choice of architecture. 

Compared with the pretrained ResNet-18, the macro recall of ResNet-18 trained from scratch is 0.4 percentage points lower for the clarifiers and 1.6 percentage points higher for the aerobic zones in Experiment~1. In Experiment~2, it is 3.5 percentage points higher for the clarifiers and 16.7 percentage points lower for the aerobic zones. Pretraining therefore made little difference at a known facility, or for the clarifiers at a new facility. However, the pretrained ResNet-18 performed substantially better on the aerobic zones at a new facility, with a macro recall of 0.517 against 0.350 and a mean AUC of 0.694 against 0.510. The aerobic zone model trained from scratch has the lowest macro recall and mean AUC of all architectures at a new facility.

\section{Qualitative Analysis: Grad-CAM}
\label{sec:results_gradcam}

The Grad-CAM method and example selection described in Section~\ref{sec:exp_gradcam} were applied to the ConvNeXt-Tiny clarifier model tested on Fisantekraal, and the DenseNet-121 aerobic zone model tested on Waterval (Figure~\ref{fig:res_cm_examples}). The last feature blocks of these networks have a spatial size of $7\times7$ and $3\times7$ respectively, so the heatmaps are coarse.

\subsection{Clarifiers}
\label{sec:results_gradcam_clarifiers}

Figure~\ref{fig:gradcam_clarifier} shows the clarifier examples. The clarifier labels are based on the surface inside the tank (Chapter~\ref{chap:systemmethods}). The rim and the masked background appear in every clarifier image, regardless of class, so on their own they carry no information about the state of the clarifier. For the correctly classified \textbf{Dysfunctional} clarifier, most emphasis is placed on the green surface, which is as expected. For the \textbf{Scum} clarifier, it is placed on the uneven patches near the centre, which is also as expected. For the \textbf{Empty} clarifier, the highest weight lies on the shadow at the top of the tank and on the rim and background next to it. The shadow appears to be cast by the wall onto the floor of the drained tank, so the weight on it is expected, but the weight on the rim and background is not. For the \textbf{Functional} clarifier, the highest weights lie on the rim and the background at the top and bottom of the tank, where the bridge meets the rim, with low weights on the black surface. This is not as expected, since the label requires the whole surface to be black. Part of the weight on the rim may be caused by the coarse heatmap.

Most of the clarifier heatmaps highlight one or two small regions rather than a large part of the surface. A possible cause is the global max pooling of ConvNeXt-Tiny (Section~\ref{sec:exp_architectures}), which passes only the largest activation of each feature map to the classifier head.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.65\textwidth]{figures/gradcam_clarifier.png}
    \caption{Grad-CAM heatmaps of the ConvNeXt-Tiny clarifier model, for test images from Fisantekraal, which was held out during training. Top: one correctly classified clarifier per class. Bottom: one clarifier from each of the four most common mistakes. Every heatmap is calculated for the assigned class, and $p$ is the softmax probability of that class. Red regions contributed the most to the classification.}
    \label{fig:gradcam_clarifier}
\end{figure}

In the \textbf{Scum} clarifier classified as \textbf{Functional} ($p = 0.97$), the lighter surface around the centre of the tank receives little weight, and the highest weights lie on the rim at the top and bottom of the tank, as for the correctly classified \textbf{Functional} clarifier. The \textbf{Functional} class requires the whole surface to be black, but the rim is not part of the surface, so it says nothing about the colour of the surface. The model therefore relied on a region that carries no information about the class, and largely ignored the lighter surface that makes this a \textbf{Scum} clarifier. In the \textbf{Functional} clarifier classified as \textbf{Scum}, the highest weight lies on a small region of the surface near the left edge of the tank, which is slightly lighter than the rest of the surface. The weight lies on the surface, so the location is appropriate, and a lighter region is the kind of feature that the \textbf{Scum} label is based on.

Of the 17 \textbf{Dysfunctional} clarifiers at Fisantekraal, 9 were misclassified. For the clarifier classified as \textbf{Functional}, the weight is spread over the rim and the surface, with the highest weight on the rim. The surface of this clarifier is pale grey rather than the darker green of the correctly classified example. The weight on the rim is not appropriate, for the reason given above, and the surface shows none of the green that the \textbf{Dysfunctional} label requires. For the clarifier classified as \textbf{Scum}, the highest weight lies on a dark spot on the surface below the centre of the tank. This location is appropriate. The two labels also overlap: a green surface is not uniformly black, so a \textbf{Dysfunctional} clarifier also fits the definition of \textbf{Scum} (Chapter~\ref{chap:systemmethods}).

\subsection{Aerobic Zones}
\label{sec:results_gradcam_aerobic}

Figure~\ref{fig:gradcam_aerobic} shows the aerobic zone examples. The aerobic zone labels are based on how much of the surface shows the leopard print pattern (Chapter~\ref{chap:systemmethods}). For the correctly classified \textbf{Functional} zone ($p = 0.80$), the heatmap covers the centre of the zone, where the leopard print pattern is visible, which is as expected. For the correctly classified \textbf{Dysfunctional} zone ($p = 0.84$), it lies on the smooth surface in the right half of the zone, which is also as expected. The correctly classified \textbf{Suboptimal} zone has $p = 0.40$, slightly above the $0.33$ of a random guess between three classes, and its heatmap lies on a darker band at the lower right of the zone. The \textbf{Suboptimal} label depends on the fraction of the surface without leopard print, and also covers zones whose state is unclear, so a single band is not expected to be sufficient evidence for it. The low $p$ is consistent with the results at Waterval, where only 1 of the 92 \textbf{Suboptimal} zones is classified correctly (Figure~\ref{fig:res_cm_examples}). In several heatmaps, part of the weight lies on the black bars added by the padding (Section~\ref{sec:exp_architectures}). The last feature block has only three rows, so the bottom row covers both the bar and the lower edge of the zone, and these heatmaps do not show whether the model uses the bars. This would not be appropriate, since the bars are added by the padding and are not part of the aerobic zone.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.7\textwidth]{figures/gradcam_aerobic_zone.png}
    \caption{Grad-CAM heatmaps of the DenseNet-121 aerobic zone model, for test images from Waterval, which was held out during training. Top: one correctly classified aerobic zone per class. Bottom: one aerobic zone from each of the three most common mistakes. Every heatmap is calculated for the assigned class, and $p$ is the softmax probability of that class. Red regions contributed the most to the classification.}
    \label{fig:gradcam_aerobic}

The \textbf{Functional} zone classified as \textbf{Dysfunctional} ($p = 0.70$) is blurred, and no leopard print pattern is visible on its surface. Its heatmap lies on the centre of the zone. The location is appropriate, and since no pattern is visible, the image fits the \textbf{Dysfunctional} definition, so the mistake is more likely caused by the image quality or a labelling error than by where the model focusses its attention. In the \textbf{Suboptimal} zone classified as \textbf{Dysfunctional} ($p = 0.90$), the heatmap lies on the left end of the zone and on a band in the right half, where the surface shows dark patches. This location is also appropriate, since a surface without leopard print is evidence for \textbf{Dysfunctional}. The two classes differ only in how much of the surface shows no leopard print, a third or more against two thirds or more (Chapter~\ref{chap:systemmethods}), which a heatmap does not show. The \textbf{Suboptimal} zone classified as \textbf{Functional} ($p = 0.52$) has a plain grey surface, and its heatmap is spread over the centre of the zone. The area is appropriate, but no pattern is visible, so the image does not support the \textbf{Functional} class. The aerobic zone labels were not reviewed (Chapter~\ref{chap:systemmethods}), so some of these mistakes may be due to labelling errors.


\section{Summary of Findings}
\label{sec:results_summary}

In this chapter we have presented the multi-class and binary results of the two experiments, the investigated effect of augmentation, and the analysed Grad-CAM heatmaps for two selected models. At a known facility, the best clarifier and aerobic zone models reach a similar macro recall. At a new facility, the clarifier models perform about as well as at a known facility, while the aerobic zone models perform considerably worse. 

When the decision threshold is selected so that 95\% of the components classified as defective (Not-Ok) in the development set are correctly identified, the test recall at a known facility is between 0.951 and 0.974 for the clarifiers and between 0.924 and 0.950 for the aerobic zones. The models with the highest specificity misclassify approximately a third of the OK clarifiers and 17\% of the OK aerobic zones as Not-OK. At a new facility, the mean test recall is between 0.867 and 0.902 for the clarifiers and 0.562 to 0.864 for the aerobic zones, indicating that a threshold chosen based on known facilities does not reach the corresponding target recall when presented with images from a new facility. The Grad-CAM heatmaps of most correctly classified images identify the features that the human labels are based on. However, for some clarifier classes they lie largely on the rim of the tank, which should be uninformative.

In the next chapter we will draw conclusions from these results, discuss the limitations of the project, and suggest future work.
