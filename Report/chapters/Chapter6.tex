\mychapter{Results}{Results}{}\label{chap:results}

\section{Overview}
\label{sec:results_overview}

The results are presented for two situations. In the first, which is the usual situation in the intended use of the system, earlier images of a facility have been relabelled and are part of the training data. Experiment~1 estimates the performance in this situation, since all of its test images were taken after its training images (Section~\ref{sec:exp_mixed}). In the second, which occurs when a facility is added, none of its images have been labelled. Experiment~2 estimates the performance in this situation.

Section~\ref{sec:results_multiclass} gives the multi-class results, Section~\ref{sec:results_binary} the binary OK / Not-OK results at a threshold chosen for a Not-OK recall of 95\% on the development set, and Section~\ref{sec:results_gradcam} the Grad-CAM heatmaps of the two selected models. The full results are given in Appendix~\ref{appen:additional_results}. In every table, the best value in every column is shown in bold.

Table~\ref{tab:res_multiclass} shows that the macro F1 score of the aerobic zones decreases from Experiment~1 to Experiment~2 for every architecture, by between 16.7 and 32.7 percentage points. For the clarifiers, the change ranges from a decrease of 6.4 to an increase of 6.7 percentage points, so the clarifier models perform about as well at a new facility as on new images of a known facility. The aerobic zone models therefore generalise worse to a new facility than the clarifier models. In Experiment~1, the \textbf{Empty} class has the lowest clarifier F1 score of every architecture (Appendix~\ref{appen:res_mixed}). DenseNet-121 has the highest clarifier F1 score in Experiment~1 and ConvNeXt-Tiny in Experiment~2, while EfficientNet-B0 has the highest aerobic zone F1 score in Experiment~1 and DenseNet-121 in Experiment~2. In Experiment~2, the standard deviation of the clarifier F1 score across facilities (0.062 to 0.098) is of the same size as the differences between the pretrained architectures (0.668 to 0.775), so the test facility has about as large an effect on the result as the architecture. ResNet-18 trained from scratch has the lowest macro F1 score for both components in both experiments. Compared with the pretrained ResNet-18, its clarifier F1 score is 6.5 and 0.6 percentage points lower in Experiments~1 and~2, and its aerobic zone F1 score is 1.4 and 10.4 percentage points lower. Part of this difference is due to tuning (Section~\ref{subsec:defaults}), but since pretraining gave a higher score in every case, it is worth using.

\section{Qualitative Analysis: Grad-CAM}
\label{sec:results_gradcam}

The Grad-CAM method and example selection described in Section~\ref{sec:exp_gradcam} were applied to the ConvNeXt-Tiny clarifier model, tested on Fisantekraal, and the DenseNet-121 aerobic zone model, tested on Waterval (Figure~\ref{fig:res_cm_examples}). The last feature blocks of these networks have a spatial size of $7\times7$ and $3\times7$ respectively, so the heatmaps are coarse.

\subsection{Clarifiers}
\label{sec:results_gradcam_clarifiers}

Figure~\ref{fig:gradcam_clarifier} shows the clarifier examples. For the correctly classified \textbf{Dysfunctional} and \textbf{Scum} clarifiers, the highest weights lie on the surface inside the tank: on the green surface, and on the uneven patches near the centre, respectively. For the \textbf{Empty} clarifier, the highest weight lies on the shadow at the top of the tank and on the rim and background next to it. For the \textbf{Functional} clarifier, the highest weights lie on the rim and the background at the top and bottom of the tank, where the bridge meets the rim, with low weights on the black surface.

Most of the ConvNeXt-Tiny heatmaps highlight one or two small regions rather than a large part of the surface. A possible cause is the global max pooling of ConvNeXt-Tiny (Section~\ref{sec:exp_architectures}), which passes only the largest activation of each feature map to the classifier head.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.85\textwidth]{figures/gradcam_clarifier.png}
    \caption{Grad-CAM heatmaps of the ConvNeXt-Tiny clarifier model, for test images from Fisantekraal, which was held out during training. Top: one correctly classified clarifier per class. Bottom: one clarifier from each of the four most common mistakes. Every heatmap is calculated for the assigned class, and $p$ is the softmax probability of that class. Red regions contributed the most to the classification.}
    \label{fig:gradcam_clarifier}
\end{figure}

In the \textbf{Scum} clarifier classified as \textbf{Functional} ($p = 0.97$), the lighter surface around the centre of the tank receives little weight, and the highest weights lie on the rim at the top and bottom of the tank, as for the correctly classified \textbf{Functional} clarifier. The class requires the whole surface to be black, but the rim does not show this. In the \textbf{Functional} clarifier classified as \textbf{Scum}, the highest weight lies on a small region of the surface near the left edge of the tank, which is slightly lighter than the rest of the surface.

Of the 17 \textbf{Dysfunctional} clarifiers at Fisantekraal, 9 were misclassified. For the clarifier classified as \textbf{Functional}, the weight is spread over the rim and the surface, with the highest weight on the rim. The surface of this clarifier is pale grey rather than the darker green of the correctly classified example. For the clarifier classified as \textbf{Scum}, the highest weight lies on a dark spot on the surface below the centre of the tank.

\subsection{Aerobic Zones}
\label{sec:results_gradcam_aerobic}

Figure~\ref{fig:gradcam_aerobic} shows the aerobic zone examples. For the correctly classified \textbf{Functional} zone ($p = 0.80$), the heatmap covers the centre of the zone, where the leopard print pattern is visible. For the correctly classified \textbf{Dysfunctional} zone ($p = 0.84$), it lies on the smooth surface in the right half of the zone. The correctly classified \textbf{Suboptimal} zone has $p = 0.40$, slightly above the $0.33$ of a random guess between three classes, and its heatmap lies on a darker band at the lower right of the zone. In several heatmaps, part of the weight lies on the black bars added by the padding (Section~\ref{sec:exp_architectures}). The last feature block has only three rows, so the bottom row covers both the bar and the lower edge of the zone, and these heatmaps do not show whether the model uses the bars.

    \caption{Grad-CAM heatmaps of the DenseNet-121 aerobic zone model, for test images from Waterval, which was held out during training. Top: one correctly classified aerobic zone per class. Bottom: one aerobic zone from each of the three most common mistakes. Every heatmap is calculated for the assigned class, and $p$ is the softmax probability of that class. The \textbf{Functional} zone classified as \textbf{Dysfunctional} ($p = 0.70$) is blurred, and no leopard print pattern is visible on its surface. Its heatmap lies on the centre of the zone. In the \textbf{Suboptimal} zone classified as \textbf{Dysfunctional} ($p = 0.90$), the heatmap lies on the left end of the zone and on a band in the right half, where the surface shows dark patches. The \textbf{Suboptimal} zone classified as \textbf{Functional} ($p = 0.52$) has a plain grey surface, and its heatmap is spread over the centre of the zone. A zone is \textbf{Suboptimal} when a third or more of its surface shows no leopard print (Chapter~\ref{chap:systemmethods}), and in neither \textbf{Suboptimal} example is a pattern clearly visible. The aerobic zone labels were not reviewed (Chapter~\ref{chap:systemmethods}), so some of these mistakes may be caused by the labels.

\subsection{Summary of Observations}
\label{sec:results_gradcam_summary}

For the correctly classified \textbf{Dysfunctional} and \textbf{Scum} clarifiers and \textbf{Functional} and \textbf{Dysfunctional} aerobic zones, the heatmaps lie on the features that the labels are based on. For the \textbf{Functional} and \textbf{Empty} clarifiers, they lie largely on the rim and the background, which do not indicate the state of the clarifier. The most common clarifier mistake, a \textbf{Scum} clarifier classified as \textbf{Functional}, is also based on the rim. When the surface shows little evidence, as in the blurred or plain aerobic zones, the heatmaps spread over the zone. These observations are based on a few examples.


\section{Summary of Findings}
\label{sec:results_summary}

In this chapter we have presented the multi-class and binary results of the two experiments, and the Grad-CAM heatmaps of the two selected models. At a known facility, the best clarifier and aerobic zone models reach a similar macro F1 score. At a new facility, the clarifier models perform about as well as at a known facility, while the aerobic zone models perform considerably worse. The differences between the pretrained architectures are of the same size as the differences between the held-out facilities, and ResNet-18 trained from scratch performs worst in both experiments. Most \textbf{Suboptimal} aerobic zones are not identified, even at a known facility.

When the threshold is set so that 95\% of the Not-OK components in the development set are flagged, the test recall at a known facility is 0.959 to 0.974 for the clarifiers and 0.881 to 0.950 for the aerobic zones, and about a third of the OK clarifiers and 15\% of the OK aerobic zones are also flagged by the best models. At a new facility, the mean test recall is 0.867 to 0.916 for the clarifiers and 0.692 to 0.864 for the aerobic zones, so a threshold chosen on known facilities does not reach the target recall at a new facility. The Grad-CAM heatmaps of most correctly classified images lie on the features that the labels are based on, but for some clarifier classes they lie largely on the rim of the tank.

In the next chapter we will draw conclusions from these results, discuss the limitations of the project, and suggest future work.
